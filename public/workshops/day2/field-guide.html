Part 2

The methods, one idea per plate

Each widget isolates one idea from the labs. Move the sliders before you read the takeaway.

Honest evaluation: rolling origin

Models only ever see the past. Tuning happens on the validation window; the 14 test weeks are scored once. In Lab 01 the two CV folds scored 2.35 and 1.27: fold-to-fold differences are mostly the data, not the model, which is why we compare models on the same fold.

RMSSE: my error ÷ the error of copying last year

Below 1 beats the yardstick, above 1 loses to it. Because it is a ratio, a 20-unit item and a 20,000-unit item count the same.

Global models learn from everyone's windows

A local model (ETS) sees about 90 weeks of one series. A global model (LightGBM, NHITS) sees windows from all 2,000 series, so a slow item can borrow patterns from similar items.

NHITS: coarse to fine, stack by stack

Illustration on the smooth example series. Each stack looks at a pooled (blurred) input and outputs a few knots that are interpolated; the next stack models what is left.

The first stack draws the slow shape with a handful of points, the last fills in the weekly wiggles. Cheap, and robust on short noisy series. Lab 01: NHITS 1.193, Lab 03 with covariates on the median series 0.56.

Quantile (pinball) loss

Under-forecasting costs τ per unit, over-forecasting 1−τ. The forecast that minimises it is the τ-quantile of demand. NHITS_MQ learns five of these at once: the best intervals of the day.

Why per-series conformal tops out near 75 %

Simulated with 3,000 series. With n scores the margin is the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉-th largest; if that rank does not exist, the best you can do is the largest, which a new error beats 1 time in n+1. Three windows give at most 75 %. Lab 06 measured 67–68 % and 74–75 %. Pooling scores across series gives 80 % and 93 %.

Reconciliation: whom do you believe?

BottomUp trusts the items, TopDown trusts the total and splits it by the forecasts' proportions. MinTrace moves every number as little as possible, weighted by how reliable each one has been. Raise "how noisy items are" and MinTrace leans towards the total. In Lab 05 MinT-shrink was coherent and lost nothing at the items (1.251 vs 1.253).

Elo / Bradley–Terry: from duels to a rating

Every series is a match; lower RMSSE wins. NHITSx (1131) vs Naive (921): 210 points, so NHITSx wins about 77 % of series.

Ensembles: mistakes that cancel

Averaging three equally good models cuts the error by a factor that depends only on how alike their mistakes are. Different families (NHITS with and without covariates, LightGBM) disagree more, so their average wins: 1.25 vs 1.31 on held-out series.

Part 3

The results

Mean RMSSE over all series unless noted; lower is better. The seed-to-seed noise floor is about 0.009, so differences below about 0.01 are a tie.

The final leaderboard

Ensemble rows here include the half of the series that chose them, so they look better than they are. On the held-out half the top-3 average scores 1.25 vs 1.31 for the best single model and 1.36 for Day 1's LightGBM.

The day as a journey (mean RMSSE)

The biggest single step is Day 1's: going global with covariates (1.53 → 1.17). Day 2 adds a few percent more, and the cheapest of those few percent is averaging.

Lab 01 · accuracy vs training time

Out of the box no neural model beats LightGBM, and they take 10–20× longer to fit.

Lab 02 · what tuning buys

The loss is the one setting that matters (MSE 1.51 vs MAE 1.26). Eight Optuna trials cost 15× the compute for 1.6 %, about two noise floors.

Lab 03 · do the models respond to a promotion?

Direction right, size timid. The raw +82 % is an upper bound: promotions coincide with season and price cuts.

Lab 03 · covariates: mean vs typical series

NHITS without covariates wins the mean; NHITSx wins 61 % of series, the median and WAPE. Intermittent items flip the mean.

Lab 05 · reconciliation: gain or loss at each level

Ratio to the incoherent base forecasts (below 1 = better). MinT-ols is cut off at 3× for readability; at the items it is 5.7× worse.

Lab 06 · do the intervals cover what they promise?

Only quantile loss and pooled conformal reach the target. Per-series conformal is capped by n = 3.

Lab 07 · who wins where (mean RMSSE by demand type)

No model wins everywhere. NHITSx takes smooth, erratic and lumpy; the model without covariates wins intermittent.

Lab 07 · two rankings: mean RMSSE vs head-to-head rating

Lines that cross are models the mean and the duels disagree on. NHITSx is 2nd on the mean but 1st in the duels: it wins many ordinary series by a little and loses some intermittent ones by a lot.
